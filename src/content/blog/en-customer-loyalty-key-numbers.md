Numbers don't lie. When you understand the economics of customer loyalty, the question isn't whether to invest in it — it's how to do it right.

Here are the 7 figures that should shape your strategy.

## 1. Acquiring a new customer costs 5x more than retaining one

This is the most cited stat in marketing, and it holds up. Between advertising, promotions, and onboarding time, a new customer is expensive. A loyal customer already knows your business, trusts you, and comes back on their own.

## 2. Increasing retention by 5% boosts profits by 25 to 95%

This figure from Harvard Business Review shows the compounding effect of loyalty. Even a small improvement in retention has a massive impact on the bottom line — because loyal customers spend more and cost less to serve.

## 3. The top 20% of customers generate 80% of revenue

The Pareto principle applies perfectly to brick-and-mortar commerce. Your regulars are your most valuable asset. Identifying and nurturing them should be a priority.

## 4. A loyal customer spends 67% more than a new one

Bain & Company found that loyal customers spend significantly more per visit and are more open to trying new products or services. They also refer friends and family — your cheapest acquisition channel.

## 5. 68% of customers leave because they feel ignored

Not because of price. Not because of quality. Simply because they didn't feel valued. A simple "thank you", a personalized offer, or a loyalty reward goes a long way.

## 6. 50% of satisfied customers leave a review when asked at the right moment

This is the conversion rate Grow Lot users see on average when the review request is triggered at peak satisfaction — right after a positive interaction. Without a prompt, most satisfied customers never leave a review.

## 7. A business with 4.5+ stars on Google earns 28% more clicks

Your Google Business Profile rating directly impacts your visibility and traffic. Every 0.1 point gained on your average rating translates into more clicks, more calls, more visits.

## What to do with these numbers

These stats all point in the same direction: **loyalty isn't a nice-to-have, it's a growth lever**.

The merchants who grow fastest are those who build systems around loyalty — not just good intentions. Automated review requests, personalized rewards, gamification — these aren't gimmicks, they're infrastructure.
